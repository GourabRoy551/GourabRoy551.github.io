§ 01 About

A short introduction

From research and teaching in Bangladesh to an Erasmus Mundus master's across Budapest, Madrid, and Bordeaux.

I care about vision models that hold up outside a clean benchmark: models whose failure cases are visible and whose explanations are tested, not assumed.

Before joining IPCVAI, I spent two years as a Research Assistant and Adjunct Lecturer in Computer Science and Engineering at Sylhet Engineering College. I designed models, ran experiments, and prepared manuscripts across medical AI, document AI, agricultural vision, NLP, and structural computer vision. That work led to 12 peer-reviewed publications. I also mentored thesis students and helped build new image datasets such as BanglaFont45 and Bangladeshi rice varieties.

Reviewing for Informatics in Medicine Unlocked sharpened how I read research: not only looking for strong results, but asking how carefully the problem is framed, how honestly the model is evaluated, and whether the method can be trusted outside a clean benchmark.

§ 03 Research

Questions I am asking

Explainable vision and language models for medical and safety-critical workflows.

Ongoing Tutored research project · Bordeaux & UAM

What do attention and perturbation explanations reveal about BERT and CLIP?

Attention maps are often read as explanations. This project tests whether attention-based explanations hold up against perturbation-based ones when the model is not a Vision Transformer.

Supervisors Prof. Jenny Benois-Pineau (Université de Bordeaux) and Prof. Marcos Escudero-Viñolo (Universidad Autónoma de Madrid)

Read the case study
Question
Do attention-based explanations (RFEM, Ayyar et al., 2025) stay faithful beyond Vision Transformers?
Key finding
Attention-sink tokens can hide the words that actually drive BERT and CLIP predictions.
Method
Compare with whole-word Partition SHAP, deletion tests, rationale proxies, and cross-model agreement on 500 balanced SST-2 sentences.
